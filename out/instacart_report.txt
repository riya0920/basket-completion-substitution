A. DOES ADD-TO-CART ORDER CARRY SIGNAL ON REAL BASKETS?
==============================================================================
                         model  hit_at_10      mrr
         sequence (uses order)     0.0670   0.0274
bag of items (order destroyed)     0.0474   0.0185

  delta hit@10 on real baskets : +0.0196
  delta on the generator       : +0.1229

  THE DIRECTION HOLDS AND THE MAGNITUDE DOES NOT. Order carries real
  signal -- the same control, the same model, the same metric -- but
  6.3x less of it than the generator advertised.

  The generator builds baskets by walking an aisle order with a
  per-family cadence, so 'what came last' is close to deterministic in
  it. Real shoppers are messier, and the honest reading is that the
  generator's +0.1229 was an upper bound on a real effect rather than
  an estimate of one.

==============================================================================
B. DO SUBSTITUTES CO-OCCUR? THE MECHANISM, ON REAL SHOPPERS
==============================================================================
This project's sharpest finding rested on a mechanism:

  'Substitutes do not co-occur. One product per family per basket, so
   on any given trip the item LEAST likely to be beside A is A's own
   substitute.'

In the generator that is true BY CONSTRUCTION -- it places one item per
family in each basket. Whether real shoppers behave that way is a
different question, and this is the first chance to ask it.

  jaccard       group      n  mean_lift  share_cooccurring
    0.500     variant   2657     12.492              0.375
    0.500  same_aisle  89056      3.743              0.314
    0.500 cross_aisle 195484      1.337              0.172
    0.600     variant   1138     11.869              0.339
    0.600  same_aisle  90575      3.898              0.316
    0.600 cross_aisle 195484      1.337              0.172
    0.700     variant    423     10.637              0.322
    0.700  same_aisle  91290      3.966              0.316
    0.700 cross_aisle 195484      1.337              0.172

  Lift is observed co-occurrence over what independence predicts, so
  1.0 is chance. Same-aisle pairs are enumerated exhaustively; the
  cross-aisle group is sampled because there are four million of them.

  THE MECHANISM IS REFUTED ON REAL DATA, AND NOT NARROWLY.

    near-identical variants :  11.87x chance
    same aisle, different   :   3.90x
    different aisles        :   1.34x

  THE MORE SIMILAR TWO PRODUCTS ARE, THE MORE THEY CO-OCCUR. Real
  shoppers buy two yoghurt flavours, two sizes of the same milk, the
  same crisps in two bags. The generator's one-per-family rule is not a
  simplification of that behaviour, it is the reverse of it.

  SO THE GENERATOR BUILT IN THE PROPERTY IT THEN DISCOVERED. The
  presence-based switch matrix scored below chance in this project
  BECAUSE the generator guaranteed substitutes never share a basket,
  and the explanation offered for it -- 'substitutes do not co-occur' --
  is a fact about the simulator and not about shopping.

  WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT OVERTURN. It does not make the slot-switch
  definition wrong: 'A left and B arrived in the same slot' is still a
  sharper definition of a switch than 'B was nearby'. What it removes is
  the REASON given for the presence-based matrix failing. On real data a
  presence-based matrix would rank near-identical variants HIGH, so it
  might work rather well -- and this project never tested that, because
  its corpus could not.

  THE PROXY, STATED. A variant pair is two products in one aisle whose
  names share most of their words. That catches different sizes of the
  same thing and misses substitutes branded differently. The result
  holds at every threshold tried (0.5-0.7) and the direction is far
  too large to be a threshold artifact, but the proxy is doing real work
  in the argument and is not a ground-truth substitute set. Instacart
  does not have one; that is still why the generator exists.
