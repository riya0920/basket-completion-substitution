1. DOES WITHIN-BASKET ORDER CARRY SIGNAL?
==============================================================================
  sequence (uses order)            hit@10 0.2508   MRR 0.0932   n=7519
  bag of items (order destroyed)   hit@10 0.1279   MRR 0.0438   n=7519

ORDER IS WORTH +0.1229 hit@10 ON NEXT-ITEM PREDICTION.

  The control is the same model trained on SHUFFLED copies of the same
  baskets. Identical co-occurrence, identical popularity, identical
  capacity -- the only thing it cannot know is which item came last. Any
  gap is attributable to order and to nothing else, which is why the
  control is a shuffle rather than a different model.

  This is also why the generator had to change first. The previous pass
  could not run this experiment: baskets were emitted in whatever order
  the generator happened to build them, so there was no order to ignore
  and 'item2vec ignores sequence' was a statement about the model with no
  measurable consequence.

  HONEST LIMIT: first-order. The model forgets everything before the last
  item, so a basket that is obviously a cookout is represented only by
  whatever went in most recently. That is left visible rather than
  patched with an average over the basket -- averaging would quietly turn
  it back into a bag-of-items model and this comparison would stop
  meaning anything.

2. COLD START, SCORED AGAINST THE CEILING IT CANNOT REACH
==============================================================================
60 held-out products. Each method places a product the learned model has
never seen; the score is overlap@10 with what the FULL-HISTORY learned
vector would have returned.

  family_centroid    overlap@10 0.2083  (sd 0.3093)
  text_embedding     overlap@10 0.1383  (sd 0.1887)
  content_rules      overlap@10 0.1267  (sd 0.1849)

BEST FALLBACK: family_centroid at 20.8% of the learned answer.

  The ceiling is the point. Scoring cold-start methods against EACH OTHER
  answers 'which fallback is least bad'; scoring them against the vector
  the product would eventually earn answers 'how much of the
  recommendation quality is missing on day one', which is the number a
  launch team is actually asking for.

  Cold start is also the only part of a recommender that can be evaluated
  honestly with no A/B test at all, because the counterfactual is
  available: hold the product out, then look.

  The family centroid EXCLUDES the held-out product from its own
  centroid. Leaving it in is the cold-start equivalent of training on the
  test set and would make the method look excellent for a reason that
  cannot happen on day one.

  What each method assumes, in increasing order:
    family centroid  -- somebody assigned the family correctly, which in
                        a real catalogue is late and often wrong
    text embedding   -- somebody wrote a description, which is true on
                        day one because it is what goes on the page
    content rules    -- the taxonomy, and nothing else

        razors        2          29.56
     olive_oil        3          29.74
       sponges        4          31.16
      plasters        2          33.30
     soy_sauce        2          33.51
   light_bulbs        4          34.97
      vitamins        2          35.42

The slowest family's interval is 2.10x the fastest (35.4 vs 16.9 days).

  A single global due-weight applies the same urgency curve to milk and
  to light bulbs. The curve peaks at the EXPECTED interval, so a weight
  tuned on fast movers fires far too early on slow ones -- and the
  aggregate hit-rate that tuned it cannot see the difference, because it
  is dominated by the fast families that generate most of the reorders.

  Note the ceiling on this table: no family's interval can be shorter
  than the user's own trip cadence, which averages 10 days here. The
  observed spread is therefore COMPRESSED relative to real consumption --
  a household that gets through milk in three days still only buys it
  when they shop. Any per-category dial fitted on observed intervals
  inherits that compression and will under-differentiate.

  What a per-family dial does NOT fix: the interval is still a
  per-(user,item) mean shrunk to the item population, so a household
  that doubled in size last month is modelled with the average of its
  old and new cadence and is wrong in both directions.

4. THE SWITCH MATRIX ON TRIAL, ON ITS OWN
==============================================================================

  Random-guess precision on this catalogue: 0.0064
  (567 true substitute pairs out of 87990 possible pairs)

  variant                       pairs       P@50      P@200      P@500     P@1000
  raw presence counts           83144     0.0000     0.0000     0.0020     0.0010
  popularity-normalised         83144     0.0400     0.0150     0.0260     0.0290
  same-family slot switch         538     1.0000     1.0000     1.0000     1.0000

Median rank of a TRUE substitute pair, out of 83144 ranked pairs:
  raw presence counts           33603
  popularity-normalised         53072
  same-family slot switch         268

THE PRESENCE-BASED MATRIX IS UNUSABLE AT THE HEAD, WHICH IS THE ONLY
PART ANYONE SEES. Its precision@500 is 0.0020 against a random-guess rate
of 0.0064 -- BELOW CHANCE where it matters. Across the whole ranking its
true pairs sit at median 33603 of 83144, marginally better than the 41572 a
coin flip would give, and that is not a signal anyone can act on: no
product page shows the middle of a ranking.

  Popularity normalisation lifts the very top (precision@50 0.0400, about
  6x chance) and pushes the median DOWN to 53072. It is a marginal
  improvement at the head, not a rescue -- and the reason it cannot be a
  rescue is that the problem was never popularity.

  THE MECHANISM IS THE SAME STRUCTURAL FACT THAT DEFINES A SUBSTITUTE:
  SUBSTITUTES DO NOT CO-OCCUR. One product per family per basket, so on
  any given trip the item LEAST likely to be beside A is A's own
  substitute. 'B was present when A vanished' is therefore
  systematically rarer for true substitutes than for arbitrary items,
  and reweighting a signal cannot fix a signal with the wrong sign.

  A heuristic can be defeated by the very property it is trying to
  detect, and no reweighting fixes that -- the fix has to be a
  DEFINITION. A switch is not 'A left and B was around'; it is 'A left
  and B arrived in the same slot': same family, same order, same user.

  Redefined that way, precision@500 goes from 0.0020 to 1.0000.

  THAT 1.0000 IS NOT AS IMPRESSIVE AS IT LOOKS, AND SAYING SO IS THE
  POINT. The slot definition only ever emits within-family pairs, and in
  this generator every within-family pair IS a substitute -- so its
  precision is bounded at 1.0 BY CONSTRUCTION, not by learning. The
  taxonomy is doing the work.

  What the behaviour adds on top is RECALL and ORDERING: it surfaces 538
  of the 567 true pairs, which is 94.9%, and ranks them by how often the
  swap was actually observed rather than by whether it is possible. A
  merchandiser who wants to know WHICH cola to offer when this cola is
  out needs the ordering; the taxonomy alone gives them an unordered set.

  The generator cannot test the finer question -- it makes every family
  member equally substitutable, so there is no ground truth about which
  swap shoppers prefer. That is a limit of the lab, and it is the one
  place in this section where the honest answer is 'not measurable here'.

  That version uses the category taxonomy to SCOPE the search, which is
  information a retailer genuinely has. It is a smaller claim than
  'discover substitution from scratch' -- knowing two colas are both
  colas is not knowing which one a shopper will accept instead of the
  other -- and it is the claim the data supports.

  Of the raw top 500, 4.4% are true COMPLEMENTS; of the slot-switch top
  500, 0.0%. Complements are the error the presence version is built to
  make: a user who stops buying hot dogs but keeps buying buns has
  changed their shopping list, not substituted one thing for another.

  THIS SECTION EXISTS BECAUSE THE PREVIOUS PASS NEVER RAN IT. The switch
  matrix was computed, z-scored, and added to a combined scorer as one
  term among several -- where a signal that is BELOW CHANCE on its own
  is indistinguishable from a signal that is merely small. A component
  nobody evaluates alone is a component nobody can decide to remove, and
  this one was actively subtracting.

5. DIRECTIONALITY, ACTUALLY SERVED
==============================================================================
  directional P(b|a)  hit@10 0.2151
  symmetrised         hit@10 0.2240
  delta               -0.0089  (n=7519)

  THE DIRECTIONAL SCORE LOSES AS A BASKET SCORER, and the previous
  pass's plan -- 'wire the directional lift into /complete' -- would
  have made the endpoint worse.

  The asymmetry is real; the mistake was assuming a real effect must
  improve every consumer of it. Summing P(b|a) over the items already
  in the cart is a mixture of conditionals, and a globally popular b
  scores well under EVERY conditional. The symmetrised min(P(b|a),
  P(a|b)) is implicitly a SPECIFICITY filter: it demands that the
  relationship hold in both directions, which is exactly what a
  merely-popular item fails.

  So the two objects have different jobs, and the honest conclusion is
  to serve both:
    - pairwise widget ('customers who bought X also bought') uses the
      DIRECTIONAL score, because the question is genuinely directed --
      buns given hot dogs, not the reverse;
    - whole-basket completion uses the symmetric score, because
      summing conditionals rewards popularity and symmetry is the
      cheapest available correction for it.

  Measuring an improvement and not serving it is a common way a model
  change fails to reach a customer. Serving a real effect through the
  wrong consumer is a less common one and it is the failure that
  actually happened here.

  Largest asymmetries -- P(b|a) vs P(a|b):
    choc_chips_d           -> butter_c                0.2885 vs 0.1095
    butter_c               -> choc_chips_d            0.1095 vs 0.2885
    choc_chips_e           -> butter_c                0.2883 vs 0.1111
    butter_c               -> choc_chips_e            0.1111 vs 0.2883
    choc_chips_a           -> butter_c                0.2937 vs 0.1181

  The direction is FREE -- the same co-occurrence counts divided by a
  different denominator -- so symmetrising it was a modelling choice and
  not a limitation.
